SEBI's New Mutual Fund Nomination Rule 2026: What You Must Do Before September 1

If you invest in mutual funds or hold a demat account in India, there's a compliance change worth thirty seconds of your attention today. SEBI's new mutual fund nomination rule is now in force — the regulator's revised nomination framework for demat accounts and mutual fund folios took effect on September 1, 2026, and it changes how nomination works across the country.

Here's the short version. SEBI's new mutual fund nomination rule makes it compulsory for every new single-holder demat account or mutual fund folio to carry either a nominee or a formal opt-out declaration — "leave it blank" is no longer an option. If you already have an existing folio with no nominee on it, don't panic: your account will not be frozen. You'll start receiving reminders until you make a choice, though, and there are good reasons — mostly for your family's sake — to make that choice sooner rather than later.

This guide walks through what <u>SEBI's new mutual fund nomination rule</u> actually changes, who needs to act and by when, and the fastest way to add or update a nominee online, whether you invest directly, through an app, or through an advisor.

It's cross-checked directly against SEBI's official circular rather than second-hand summaries. And if you'd rather not deal with RTA logins and OTP screens yourself, our team can add your nominee for you — free — as explained further down.

What Is SEBI's New Mutual Fund Nomination Rule, Exactly?

On May 29, 2026, SEBI issued Circular No. SEBI/HO/OIAE/OIAE_IAD-3/P/CIR/2026/12676, comprehensively overhauling nomination for demat accounts and mutual fund folios. It takes effect from September 1, 2026, and it supersedes 18 older circulars on the subject — some dating back to 2002 — so this single circular is now the rulebook every AMC, RTA, and depository participant has to follow.

This isn't SEBI's first attempt. A January 2025 circular tried to revamp the same framework but created operational headaches of its own — confusing witness rules, unclear online validation steps, inconsistent forms across fund houses. The May 2026 version is SEBI's fix: simpler paperwork, clearer online methods, and a single standard form used industry-wide.

The underlying problem is a big one. Billions of rupees in shares, mutual fund units, and dividends sit unclaimed in India's securities markets, largely because investors never registered a nominee. When they pass away, families end up chasing succession certificates and court orders just to access money that was rightfully theirs. This circular is SEBI's latest attempt to close that gap.

Who Actually Needs to Act — and From When

Not every investor is affected the same way. Here's how it breaks down.

New Investors Opening an Account From September 1

If you open a new single-holder demat account or mutual fund folio on or after September 1, 2026, you cannot complete the process without making a choice. You must either:

Provide nominee details (name, relationship, and date of birth if the nominee is a minor), or
Formally opt out using SEBI's standard declaration form

Simply skipping the nomination screen, which used to be common, is no longer possible.

Existing Investors Without a Nominee

This is the part most people get wrong. If your folio or demat account already exists and has no nominee, it will not be frozen or restricted because of this circular — there's no penalty attached to the September 1 date for existing holdings.

What does change is that your fund house or depository participant must now send you reminder emails and SMS twice a year, plus a pop-up the first time you log in each day, until you either add a nominee or already have one. It's a nudge, not a lock.

Joint Account and Folio Holders

Nomination stays entirely optional for jointly held demat accounts and mutual fund folios. If joint holders do want to add or change a nominee, though, every single holder must consent — even on accounts run on an "either or survivor" basis by just one person.

Your Nominee Is Not Automatically Your Legal Heir

This is the single most misunderstood part of mutual fund nomination, and it matters more than the September 1 date itself.

Naming someone as your nominee doesn't automatically make them the final owner of your investment after you're gone. Under Indian succession law, as the Supreme Court clarified in Shakti Yezdani vs Jayanand Jayant Salgaonkar, a nominee typically holds the units as a trustee on behalf of your legal heirs — not as the outright owner. AMFI's own investor guidance for mutual funds says the same thing.

In practice, this means nomination makes the immediate transmission of your units fast and simple. It does not replace a will, and it doesn't override applicable succession law. If you want your investments to actually go to a specific person, nomination and a proper will need to work together, not one instead of the other.

How to Add or Update Your Nominee Online

Both processes take a few minutes once you know where to look.

Updating Your Mutual Fund Nomination
Log in to MF Central, the CAMS or KFintech portal (whichever RTA services your fund house), or your fund house's or advisor's own app.
Verify your identity with your PAN and an OTP.
Open the "Nominee" or "Update Nomination" section and select the folio(s) you want to update.
Enter the nominee's name, relationship to you, and date of birth (only if they're a minor). Add a percentage split if you have more than one nominee.
Confirm using the OTP sent to your registered mobile and email, an Aadhaar e-sign, or a digital signature certificate.
Save your acknowledgement — SEBI now requires one to be issued every time you nominate or make a change.
Updating Your Demat Account Nomination
Log in to your broker or depository participant's app — most platforms have a nominee section under account or profile settings.
Select "Add Nominee" or "Manage Nominees."
Enter the same core details: name, relationship, date of birth if a minor, plus an optional percentage split, mobile number, and email.
Verify with an e-sign or OTP.
Prefer paper? A physical form with a wet signature still works, and you no longer need a witness unless you're signing with a thumb impression.

If your mutual fund investments are spread across several AMCs handled by different RTAs, you'll need to repeat the update on each platform separately — one more reason many investors just hand this over to their advisor.

Demat Nomination vs Mutual Fund Nomination — Not the Same Action

Both fall under this same SEBI circular, but they are two separate records, and updating one does not automatically update the other.

Demat account nomination covers the shares, ETFs, and bonds sitting in your demat account, and is set with your depository participant (your broker).
Mutual fund nomination covers each individual folio and is set with the AMC or its RTA (CAMS/KFintech), independently of your demat holdings — even if you also hold mutual funds in demat form.

If you hold both, plan on checking — and if needed, updating — both separately.

Why Add a Nominee Now, Even Without a Freeze Deadline

Since there's no forced deadline for existing investors, it's fair to ask why bother right now. A few honest reasons:

The reminders don't stop on their own. Bi-annual SMS, email, and login pop-ups will keep coming until you actually add a nominee — opting out doesn't turn them off.
It genuinely helps your family. Without a nominee, your legal heirs typically need a succession certificate, legal heir certificate, or probate before they can claim your investments — a process that can drag on for months.
Unclaimed holdings eventually move to the IEPF. If nobody claims an investment for a long period, it can be transferred to the Investor Education and Protection Fund, adding another layer of process to reclaim it later.
It takes a few minutes, once. Compared to the alternative for your family, this is about the smallest effort-to-benefit ratio in personal finance.

Frequently Asked Questions
Will my existing mutual fund folio or demat account be frozen if I don't add a nominee by September 1, 2026?

No. SEBI's May 2026 circular does not bring back the old freeze rule. Existing folios and demat accounts without a nominee keep working normally — you'll just start receiving reminder emails, SMS, and a login pop-up until you make a choice.

Is nomination compulsory when I open a new demat account or mutual fund folio after September 1, 2026?

Yes, for single-holder accounts. You must either provide nominee details or formally submit an opt-out declaration; leaving the field blank is no longer allowed. Joint accounts are handled differently — see below.

Is nomination mandatory for jointly held mutual fund folios or demat accounts?

No, it remains optional. If joint holders do want to add, change, or remove a nominee, every holder must consent, even on accounts operated on an either-or-survivor basis.

How many nominees can I add to one mutual fund folio or demat account?

Up to three. Skip the percentage split and the holding divides equally, with any odd lot going to the first nominee listed on the form.

Do I need a witness to nominate someone on a physical form?

Not if you sign with a wet signature — that requirement has been removed. A witness (two, in fact) is only needed if you use a thumb impression instead of signing.

Does my nominee automatically become the legal owner of my mutual fund units after I pass away?

No. Per the Supreme Court's ruling in Shakti Yezdani vs Jayanand Jayant Salgaonkar and AMFI's own guidance, a nominee generally holds the investment as a trustee for your legal heirs. Nomination speeds up transmission; it doesn't replace a will.

I already opted out of nomination earlier. Will I still get reminder messages?

Yes. SEBI treats an opt-out the same as "no nomination" for reminder purposes, so the bi-annual nudges continue either way. Only adding an actual nominee stops them.

How do I add or change a mutual fund nominee online?

Log in to MF Central, CAMS, KFintech, or your broker's or advisor's platform, open the nominee section, enter the nominee's name, relationship, and date of birth if a minor, then confirm with an OTP, Aadhaar e-sign, or digital signature certificate.

Nomination is one of those tasks that's easy to postpone precisely because nothing bad happens today if you skip it. But SEBI's new mutual fund nomination rule is a useful nudge to close that gap — for new investors, it's now a required step; for everyone else, it's a five-minute task that saves your family real time and paperwork later.
